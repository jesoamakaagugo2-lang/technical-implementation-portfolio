Illustrative work sample

A customer recovery plan

A customer reports low adoption and repeated support issues. How would I turn that into an achievable plan?

Explore the plan +
  1. Understand the impact. Listen to the customer, review support history and clarify what they need the product to do.
  2. Separate the blockers. Identify access problems, training needs and product gaps. Confirm priorities with the customer.
  3. Agree the next steps. Record actions, owners, milestones and the next update time.
  4. Review progress. Check adoption and open issues against the starting point. Do not assume retention or renewal outcomes.

Illustrative work sample

Office phone system cutover

A rollout plan covering call-flow testing, fallback arrangements and customer handover.

Read the rollout plan +
  1. Map requirements. List extensions, phone numbers, business hours, greetings, ring groups and voicemail routes.
  2. Configure and test. Check registration, inbound and outbound calls, two-way audio, transfers and after-hours handling.
  3. Prepare the change. Record the previous routing, agree a go-live window and confirm who can restore the old route if calls fail.
  4. Hand over. Record test results, open issues and owners. Provide quick instructions for transfers, voicemail and reporting problems.

Illustrative work sample

Isolating an outbound call failure

An extension receives calls but cannot dial out. Begin with scope, then follow the call path.

Read the diagnostic sequence +
  1. Establish scope. Compare a known-good extension using the same destination and dial format. Record the time, symptom and any SIP response.
  2. Check routing. Review outbound permissions and dial rules. If multiple extensions fail, check the trunk and provider responses.
  3. Verify. Retest the original scenario and confirm inbound calling still works after any authorized change.
  4. Escalate with evidence. Share checks, results, sanitized logs and the next owner when the issue remains unresolved.

Illustrative work sample

An escalation engineers can act on

A user can sign in but cannot open a key feature. Capture the evidence that makes the next step possible.

See the escalation outline +
  • Expected versus actual behavior, impact, affected scope and timestamp.
  • Environment, exact error and reproducible steps.
  • Checks already completed and the result of each.
  • Sanitized screenshots or logs; exclude credentials and personal health information.
  • Priority, escalation owner and the next customer update.

Illustrative work sample

A website fails after an update

A structured walkthrough for investigating a WordPress 500 error after a plugin update.

Explore the walkthrough +
  1. Confirm affected URLs, timing, recent changes and available backups.
  2. Review PHP and web server logs; identify the relevant error and redact sensitive data.
  3. Reproduce in staging where possible. Isolate the suspected plugin and check PHP compatibility and memory limits.
  4. Apply a reversible fix with approval. Verify key pages and forms, then document the cause and prevention steps.
